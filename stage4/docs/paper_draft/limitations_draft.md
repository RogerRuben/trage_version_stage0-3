# Limitations draft

The Stage4 findings are internally comparable under the frozen scenario design, but their interpretation and external validity are bounded in the following ways.

1. **Single empirical evaluation day and setting.** The final experiment uses the Test31 Xi'an case. It does not contain repeated days, alternative cities, or independent simulation seeds from which sampling uncertainty or broad external validity could be estimated. Reported differences are deterministic scenario contrasts, not inferential population effects.

2. **Exogenous passenger acceptance.** The AV acceptance parameter (p_A) is assigned at scenario level, and order-level binary eligibility (a_o^A) is realized from a frozen common-random-number draw. The framework does not estimate individual preferences, heterogeneity, learning, opt-in behavior, prices, or welfare. Acceptance contrasts therefore describe the modeled eligibility mechanism rather than an empirically calibrated behavioral response.

3. **Analytical capability profiles.** C, M, and A are assumed nested capability/reference profiles constructed for the experiment. They are not commercial certification categories and do not represent claims about specific AV systems. A route lying within a profile envelope is an analytical compatibility result, not evidence of certified operation.

4. **Operational exposure is not safety risk.** Static, dynamic, and speed exposure measure utilization relative to frozen operational reference envelopes. They are not probabilities of accidents, failures, disengagements, or unsafe operation. Likewise, cumulative \(\Gamma\) budgets are dispatch-policy parameters rather than validated safety thresholds.

5. **Reconstructed HV supply.** HV availability is reconstructed from empirical service sessions and represents an effective operating fleet, not the city's registered fleet size. Comparisons with full-horizon AV availability therefore evaluate two deliberately different supply constructions. They should not be interpreted as a census comparison of physical fleet sizes.

6. **Simplified AV availability.** Idle AVs are modeled as available across the full horizon. The simulator does not impose charging, state-of-charge, maintenance, cleaning, depot, labor-supervision, duty-cycle, or scheduled downtime constraints. Introducing these processes could alter both temporal availability and effective service capacity.

7. **No endogenous congestion feedback.** Dispatch decisions do not feed back into a traffic-flow or network-congestion simulation. Travel and pickup predictions therefore do not reflect congestion generated by the simulated fleet itself, repositioning externalities, or network equilibrium responses.

8. **Normalized rather than monetary cost.** The cost formulation uses normalized analytical quantities. It omits calibrated wages, energy prices, vehicle ownership, depreciation, insurance, charging infrastructure, supervision, and passenger welfare. Furthermore, changing \(\eta\) changes the objective definition, so raw cost levels cannot be ranked across \(\eta\) as monetary efficiency outcomes.

9. **Conditional waiting-time metrics.** Pickup-time quantiles are calculated for matched passengers and cluster near the 300 s patience boundary. They do not describe the experience of expired requests. Service rate and patience expiration must therefore accompany all waiting-time comparisons.

10. **Incomplete temporal supply decomposition.** Epoch outputs record total available stock but not separate available-HV and available-AV inventories. The temporal evidence supports an effective-capacity interpretation, but it cannot identify how much of the service gap arises from passenger acceptance, route eligibility, temporal alignment, or vehicle-type-specific stock.

11. **Frozen dispatch and policy design.** The results cover the implemented rolling horizon, patience rule, candidate construction, capability envelopes, exposure policies, and local cost relaxation. They do not establish that these settings are globally optimal. No additional parameter search or counterfactual experiment was conducted during result analysis or drafting.

12. **Descriptive interaction contrasts.** The reported penetration–acceptance and penetration–capability interactions are deterministic differences across frozen scenarios. Without replicated uncertainty or an identification strategy, they do not support inferential or causal interaction claims.
