# Paper storyline v3

## Central chain

Nominal supply → interface-conditioned compatibility graph → instantaneous effective matching capacity → rolling realized service.

The graph depends on capability assumptions, passenger acceptance, route selection,
network/identity coverage, evidence and patience. It is not a fully observed map
of physical AV capability. Prediction supports graph construction, not a second headline.

## Empirical mechanism

The paper asks why nominal AV availability is not equivalent to effective service capacity. Same-unit gates and mixed-fleet scenarios show the conversion losses; the prediction layer supplies a decision-time, route-specific dynamic interface rather than a generic accuracy benchmark. The fixed-state ablation confirms that this interface changes feasible decision structure, but its mixed metric-level accuracy prevents an overbroad predictive-superiority claim.

## Claim hierarchy

Primary: effective-capacity conversion under operational compatibility. Secondary: separate family-specific control and local theoretical guarantees. Supporting: leakage-safe prediction is decision-relevant in frozen states; acceptance matters increasingly with AV penetration; tested ODD policies trade observed exposure and service. Boundary: no proactive rebalancing, and repositioning effect not identified.

## Final decision

`ACCEPT_FROZEN_INTERFACE_BOUNDARY / CONVERGE_MANUSCRIPT`.
Retain the 41 results but disclose the known endpoint/movement interface gap in the
main paper. Stop further Stage3 audit/repair/clustering, new gate experiments and Stage5.
No universal AV productivity penalty, repaired-network effect or decision superiority
is identified. Finish source-grounded figures, literature positioning and language.
