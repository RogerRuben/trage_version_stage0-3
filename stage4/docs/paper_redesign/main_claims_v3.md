# Main claims v3

1. Nominal active hours, instantaneous effective matching capacity and realized rolling service are distinct quantities; the trajectory pipeline supports their empirical study rather than being the central contribution.
2. AV dispatchability is represented by hard structural feasibility plus separate continuous static, dynamic, and speed-family utilization—not by a binary safety label.
3. Under the frozen interface, zero lead and 300-second pickup patience, higher baseline-normalized AV active-hour level is associated with lower service. Implemented gates remove genuine AV matching capacity, but AV-subgraph evidence does not decompose total mixed-fleet service.
4. Frozen-state evidence shows the decision-time prediction layer is decision-relevant: P versus a Train-only historical baseline changes 9/10 assignment sets, while not claiming universal prediction superiority.
5. Family-specific cumulative exposure control has explicit guarantees and prevents cross-family compensation hidden by a scalar score.
6. Full-day results are conditional on the frozen no-active-rebalancing baseline, including known endpoint/movement coverage gaps and route-selection branching. Not all exclusions are physical restrictions; no corrected-network service effect is estimated.
